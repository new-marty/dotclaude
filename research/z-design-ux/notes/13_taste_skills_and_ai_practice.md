## Q1. How do impeccable, Hallmark, taste-skill and Anthropic's frontend-aesthetics prompt work: inputs, anti-pattern lists, forcing a distinctive direction?

### Takeaway
All four share one diagnosis: a model's default is the "average of every page it has seen", so a style adjective is not enough. They differ in the lever. Anthropic's cookbook relies on a short prompt that names dimensions and bans defaults. Hallmark and impeccable add a context gate (audience, use, tone, or a PRODUCT.md) and a forced structural or visual choice. taste-skill and impeccable also use scripted randomness (a dice-deal of directions) and the product's own facts to break the category default. The consistent upstream inputs they want are: audience and situation, the single primary job, an extreme tone (not "clean and modern"), real content and assets (with absences stated), required states, platform and constraints, and explicit anti-goals. None of the four does UX work (flows, IA, state matrix) upstream; impeccable's `shape` is the closest and stops at a brief.

### Cited Findings

#### Anthropic cookbook (primary)
- Cookbook says Claude "defaults to safe choices unless explicitly encouraged otherwise" and names three strategies: guide specific design dimensions, reference design inspirations (IDE themes, cultural aesthetics) "without being overly prescriptive", and call out common defaults to avoid. — [claude-cookbooks notebook, raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/claude-cookbooks/main/coding/prompting_for_frontend_aesthetics.ipynb) (fetched via curl; saved as `cookbook_aesthetics.ipynb`)
- The prompt is a `<frontend_aesthetics>` block: "You tend to converge toward generic, 'on distribution' outputs"; four dimensions (Typography, Color & Theme, Motion, Backgrounds); an avoid list (overused fonts Inter/Roboto/Arial/system fonts; "purple gradients on white backgrounds"; "Predictable layouts and component patterns"; "Cookie-cutter design that lacks context-specific character"); and "You still tend to converge on common choices (Space Grotesk, for example) across generations." It is meant to be appended to a system prompt or CLAUDE.md. Color guidance: "Dominant colors with sharp accents outperform timid, evenly-distributed palettes." Motion guidance: one orchestrated page load beats scattered micro-interactions. — same notebook
- The notebook shows before/after screenshots for a SaaS landing page, a blog layout and an admin table, and has an "isolated prompting" section to target one dimension (typography only, etc.). It reports results only as screenshots, with no metric. — same notebook
- Note: the avoid-list itself becomes the next default (Space Grotesk named as a new convergence point), which impeccable and taste-skill both respond to (below).

#### impeccable (pbakaus/impeccable, commit dad8dbb, 2026-10-20; v4.7.0 SKILL; Apache 2.0)
- Structure: `SKILL.md` (85 lines) routes to `reference/*.md` per command (shape, init, new-work, craft-floor, critique 819 lines, document, etc.). Files read: `.veto/skills/impeccable/SKILL.md`, `reference/{shape,init,new-work,craft-floor}.md`. — [repo](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable)
- Two durable context files: PRODUCT.md (product truth) and DESIGN.md (durable visual decisions); a surface brief holds strategy for one route. "PRODUCT.md owns product truth. DESIGN.md owns durable visual decisions. A surface brief keeps strategy that belongs to one route or artifact." — `reference/new-work.md`
- `init` PRODUCT.md template sections: Platform, Stack, Users, Product Purpose, Positioning ("The product mechanism or claim a neighboring product could not truthfully copy"), Operating Context, Capabilities and Constraints, Brand Commitments, Evidence on Hand ("State absences that future work must not fabricate"), Product Principles (3-5), Accessibility & Inclusion. "Do not ask for an aesthetic direction, emotional feel, visual references, colors, typography, or style during init." Confirmed facts only; inferences must be labeled; max three questions per round; evidence from the repo is "a hypothesis, not user approval." — `reference/init.md`
- `shape` (no code): Round 1 asks purpose/problem, who and in what state of mind, primary thing to understand or do and success, "What is uniquely true here that a neighboring product or generic template could not claim?" Round 2 (only if gaps) asks real content ranges (minimum, typical, maximum), which states matter (first-run, empty, loading, error, success, permissions, overflow, expert), fidelity/breadth/interactivity, "What must remain untouched? What would make the result feel wrong even if it looked polished?", binding constraints. Brief has seven parts: job and audience (with visitor mode), outcome and proof, selected direction (structural/interaction thesis, sequence, focal moment), scope and anti-goals, states and ranges, interaction and layout "intent, not CSS", constraints and open decisions "a builder must not invent". Ends: "shape never writes code or a direction contract." — `reference/shape.md`
- Mode taxonomy sets ambition: Persuade, Operate, Read, Experience; for Operate/Read "ambition shows as precision and restraint" and known patterns "earn their place." Mode is persisted only in the surface brief. — `SKILL.md`
- Forcing a distinctive direction (new visual world): name the product's unique mechanism, audience scene and cultural home; note "the page this category always ships and its predictable opposite" and keep both out; list seven concrete visual systems/artifacts/rituals from the audience's world; run a script `concept-seed` that deals the direction ("the roll is what keeps every run from converging on the category default"; skipping it is "a contract violation"). A "standing exit" lets the user choose the category standard, which the agent never recommends. Whole-surface work inside an established world derives 5-7 materially different structures and deals 3 to the user. — `reference/new-work.md`
- Color/type commitments: choose a color strategy before colors (Restrained, Committed, Full palette, Drenched); "Dark or light is never a default: write one sentence of physical scene"; a named list of training-data default fonts (Fraunces, Playfair Display, Space Grotesk, Inter-as-display, DM Sans, Plus Jakarta Sans, etc.) needs "a reason no other face could satisfy". Calibration paragraph lists four recurring AI looks (cream + serif + terracotta; near-black + one neon accent; broadsheet-editorial hairlines/italic serif/tracked mono labels; saturated ink with hard offset shadows). Self-check: "if someone could guess your aesthetic from the category alone, or from category-plus-avoidance, rework." — `reference/new-work.md`
- "The brief wins": pinned aesthetics are honored even when they conflict with a saturated-pattern warning; "Redirecting a clear brief toward your taste is failure." — `SKILL.md`
- Direction contract (six blocks, ~150 words, kept in the surface brief and never copied into shipped source): THESIS (the idea the surface owns and the category-default arrangement it refuses), OWN-WORLD, STORY, FIRST VIEWPORT, plus further blocks. — `reference/new-work.md`
- Craft floor (loaded just before UI edits) has a Verify list (contrast 4.5:1, spacing, type measure 65-75ch, one authored motion moment, states: hover/disabled/loading/error/empty "plus real content, working controls, responsive composition, keyboard focus", browser surfaces such as selection/caret/scrollbars, copy, coverage of every brief requirement) and a Refuse list: same-size icon+heading+text cards as page structure, hero-metric template, eyebrow above a heading ("a ban, not a default: no brief earns it back"), section numbers, gradient text, glass as decoration, thick colored side borders, hard offset shadows outside a neobrutalist world, monospace as costume, system display fonts, emoji as icons. "The floor holds the mechanics; it never picks the direction." — `reference/craft-floor.md`
- Treats the "existing look" as evidence for redesign; "A section, component, feature, or state inside an established surface inherits that surface. Never turn a local addition into a new identity exercise." — `reference/new-work.md`

#### Hallmark (Nutlope/hallmark, commit 13ac0ec, 2026-08-06; v1.1.0; MIT)
- Sources disagree on counts: README-derived summaries say 20 or 22 themes and 57 or 65 slop gates; the SKILL.md I read says 21 named themes. A commentary notes the gates "are prompt-encoded rules, not a compile-time or automated verification." — [search summary](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/nutlope/hallmark), SKILL.md
- Verbs: default build, `audit` (score, no edit), `redesign`, `study <screenshot|URL>` (extract "DNA": macrostructure, archetypes, type pairing, colour anchor). Differentiator claimed: "structural variety, not just visual variety"; two briefs should not share "the same hero → 3-feature → CTA → footer rhythm". Picks a macrostructure before a theme and logs picks in `.hallmark/log.json` to rotate. — `skills/hallmark/SKILL.md`
- Design-context gate asks, once, in one message, three things: Audience, Use case ("the one action the page should drive"), Tone ("Pick an extreme: editorial, brutalist, soft, utilitarian, luxury, playful, technical, austere. 'Clean and modern' isn't a tone."). The user may say "go ahead"; then it must disclose its inferences in one sentence and stamp them in a CSS comment. "There is no 'the brief looks complete' exception... The cost of asking is one extra message; the cost of guessing wrong is a whole rebuild." — `SKILL.md` §1
- Six cross-verb disciplines: pre-emit self-critique on six axes (Philosophy, Hierarchy, Execution, Specificity, Restraint, Variety; below 3 triggers revision); "Honest copy, no fabricated content" (invented stats, testimonials, logos are slop; use placeholders); locked tokens (every color/font via a named token); no re-drawn browser/phone chrome; verified at 320/375/414/768 widths; roman headings, no italic emphasis words. — `SKILL.md`
- Component scope: an interactive component must ship "all 8 states: default, hover, focus-visible, active, disabled, loading, error, success" plus a demo wrapper that renders each state. — `SKILL.md`
- Anti-pattern registry entries come with "Fix" lines (purple-gradient hero, Inter-everywhere, 3-column feature grid, card-in-card, gradient headline). — `references/anti-patterns.md`
- Output contract: tokens in `:root` or tokens file, named by semantic role ("`--color-ink`, not `--color-black`"), existing global stylesheet is append-only, top-of-file comment naming genre, tone, anchor hue, structural fingerprint. Scope: "It will not invent product copy... pick a brand identity... build logic." — `references/contract.md`
- Optional portable `design.md` emitted only when the user says "lock the system"; never auto-emitted, not overwritten if present. — `references/design-md.md`
- Boundary: Hallmark expects the user to supply copy and brand; it is a visual layer.

#### taste-skill (Leonxlnx/taste-skill, commit 63aba5f, 2026-10-20; skill name `design-taste-frontend`, "tasteskill v2"; MIT)
- Premise: "A page built by a model usually looks like the average of every page the model has seen... Nothing in it is wrong and nothing in it was decided." Four mechanisms: a script (`draw.mjs`) deals 3 of 20 researched directions from a library distilled from "247 curated gallery sites (One Page Love, Awwwards Sites of the Day)"; the idea must come from the subject; a house style with no ornaments measured by a real-browser inspector (`inspect.mjs`); a look-and-fix loop on screenshots. Requires Node 22+ and Chrome. — `skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md`
- Inputs it wants: subject, audience, "the one thing the page must get someone to do", stack, existing brand, "above all what imagery truly exists" ("Do not plan around pictures you do not have"). If the request is vague "decide and state your reading in one line instead of asking. Ask only when a missing fact blocks the work." (opposite of Hallmark's always-ask.) — `SKILL.md` §1
- "Inventory the subject": 8-12 things that belong to this client and no competitor (name and meaning, address and street, numbers in the brief, constraints, materials, rituals and hours, printed artefacts). Claim: "the page that won each brief was built from an item on such a list" in blind tests (author's own testing; not independently verified). — `SKILL.md`
- "Write the argument" before design: sections as claims with proof, one line each; delete any section with no claim; avoids the stock hero-logos-features-testimonial-pricing-CTA sequence. — `SKILL.md` §2
- Idea sentence: "On this page, [something that belongs to the subject] becomes [the device the direction calls for]." Competitor test: put the nearest competitor in the sentence; if it still works the idea is category-level. "Clean and modern is not an idea." Also "one live thing": one interaction made from the idea. — `SKILL.md` §4
- Tells registry is dated in three waves (2023-24, 2025, 2025-26) and "never names a replacement font, colour or component: every published 'use this instead' list has become the next wave of tells within months. Replacements here are procedures." Third wave: "warm it up and make it editorial" (cream ground + terracotta + serif display) became "the most recognisable generated look of all." Statuses banned/warned. Copy tells and structural tells included. — `references/tells.md`
- Warning learned in testing: removing ornaments alone makes pages "read as thin"; blind jurors preferred "a richer page with tells over an empty page without them", so substance (real imagery, larger type, full content) must replace them. — `SKILL.md`
- Critique file: fourteen look-passes on screenshots (one-second/five-second test, squint, "cover the logo" competitor swap, count distinct left edges, space and frame, type, colour proportions, images). "Use it on the screenshots, never on the code." — `references/critique.md`
- Scope split: separate `references/app-screens.md` (1,199 lines) for dashboards/admin/settings; one third-party listing says the skill targets landing pages and not data-heavy product UI, which conflicts with the app-screens file existing in the current version. — [ui-skills listing via search](https://www.ui-skills.com/skills/leonxlnx/taste-skill) vs `skills/taste-skill/references/app-screens.md`
- Changelog 2.1.2: the skill silently failed to load in Claude Code because an unquoted `: ` broke YAML frontmatter; a strict frontmatter test was added. — `CHANGELOG.md`

### Inferences
- What the downstream skills consume, ranked by how much each reduces generic output (my synthesis): (1) a stated, extreme tone plus one sentence of audience/use scene; (2) the "uniquely true / competitor test" fact set (what this product has that no neighbor has); (3) real content and an explicit list of absent assets (so nothing is fabricated); (4) page/screen mode (Persuade/Operate/Read/Experience, or page vs app screen) because it flips the rules; (5) states and content ranges; (6) explicit anti-goals ("would feel wrong even if polished"); (7) binding brand/stack constraints.
- Both impeccable and Hallmark refuse to take visual direction from the user's adjectives during product capture; impeccable even forbids asking for it in `init`. An upstream UX skill should therefore hand over product/behavior truth and a mode, and leave visual world selection to the downstream skill, or pass direction only as an explicit "pinned" constraint (impeccable: "The brief wins").
- Because a published "use this instead" list becomes the next default (taste-skill's third wave; Anthropic's own Space Grotesk note), the handoff should not specify fonts/palettes as replacements unless they are real brand constraints. Pass derived facts and procedures, not a style menu.
- Hallmark's "always ask three questions, allow 'go ahead' with disclosure" and taste-skill's "state your reading in one line, ask only if blocked" are two opposite policies; impeccable sits between (1 round of 2-3 questions, label inferences). The upstream skill can reuse the disclosure pattern: every inferred field is labeled inferred.
- The downstream skills' state lists (8 states; hover/disabled/loading/error/empty) are checks on built output. They have no screen inventory to check against, so upstream state-per-screen output is the gap neither side fills.

## Q2. Visual-direction methods (Dan Mall element collages and "this, not that", style tiles, Curtis, Frost)

### Takeaway
Primary support exists only for element collages and the manifesto/visual-inventory practice. I found no source for a Dan Mall "this, not that" adjectives exercise (it may exist in a talk or his book/newsletter I did not reach). I could not retrieve Nathan Curtis's "when to start a design system" or tiers pieces (403 on Medium; search returned only bios).

### Cited Findings
- Mall coined "element collage": a deliverable combining type, color, icons, imagery and components to show how a style could apply; he began after noticing client brand elements (shapes, hearts, books, bubbles) in print work and sent all pieces, not only the strongest, to start a discussion about what captured the brand. — [Style Guides podcast, Dan Mall](http://styleguides.io/podcast/dan-mall/) (via search result summary; page not opened)
- Collage framing sets the expectation "that what we're looking at isn't a final design but rather an assembly of disparate pieces without specific logic or order." — same podcast (search summary); consistent with [Diagram](https://www.wearediagram.com/blog/using-element-collages-to-improve-the-design-process) in note 04.
- Mall's EAE 2015 talk notes: in his Sketch stage he arranges designed elements down a page "in no particular order" showing "all those hidden layers"; in the Plan stage "Mall writes a manifesto before designing anything"; "Creative direction contains a point of view, a perspective"; before comps he takes "a visual inventory of sites with different approaches to color, concept, and tone." The notes do not mention adjectives or "this, not that." — [Hooked on Code notes](https://hookedoncode.com/2015/02/so-what-do-i-make-by-dan-mall-notes-from-an-event-apart-talk/) (secondary notes by an attendee)
- A search for "this not that" attributed to Mall returned nothing on that exercise. — WebSearch results, no source
- Mall quote reported: something can be "really well designed and really poorly art directed, and vice versa" (secondary, search summary). — [search result](https://www.creativebloq.com/dan-mall-defeating-apathy-1134172) (not opened)

### Inferences
- A "manifesto + visual inventory before comps" step is a documented Mall practice that maps onto upstream UX: writing a point of view (what this product stands for, what it refuses) is cheap and text-based, so an agent can produce it, whereas collages need visual output.
- "This not that" as a technique (pairing each desired attribute with its rejected neighbor) is consistent with how impeccable's "THESIS: ... the category-default arrangement it refuses" and Hallmark's "pick an extreme" work, but I did not find it attributed to Mall, so the skill should not cite him for it.

## Q3. How do professionals use reference galleries without copying (Mobbin, Refero, Godly, footer.design, cta.gallery, 404s.design, bentogrids.com)?

### Takeaway
Little first-hand designer writing surfaced. What exists is vendor comparison pages and a few how-to posts; they converge on "study the problem and structure, not the screens" but do not give a validated method. Do not present gallery use as an evidenced practice.

### Cited Findings
- Mobbin is described as a library of real app screens and flows used for "pattern discovery... how other teams solved the same problem before committing to your own approach"; Refero as stronger for web-first/SaaS/landing pages; library sizes and prices disagree across pages and several are vendor-authored. — [Lazyweb comparison](https://www.lazyweb.com/compare/mobbin-vs-refero.md) (vendor with a competing product), [UX Magic review](https://uxmagic.ai/blog/mobbin-review-2026)
- Reviewers note Mobbin shows flows, states, animations and timing; mostly mobile; paid. Godly is a free link collection that points to the live source. Awwwards suits premium brand execution but "less practical for SaaS or B2B". — [Streamline Icons roundup](https://blog.streamlinehq.com/best-design-inspiration-websites/) and [UXCEL](https://uxcel.com/blog/top-7-resources-for-ux-ui-designers-for-meaningful-design-inspiration) via search summary (pages not opened)
- A skill guide lists a study sequence for award sites: identify the technique, understand its purpose, analyze timing, map to implementation, adapt to context; a practitioner guide says copying a reference gets "a worse version of someone else's work" and advises blending qualities of several. — [skills.sh elite-inspiration](https://skills.sh/rshvr/elite-web-design/elite-inspiration) and [UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/exploring-design-inspiration-a-comparative-analysis-of-three-inspiring-websites-945be37d3dc4) via search summary (not opened; low authority)
- Hallmark's `study` verb extracts structural "DNA" from a screenshot/URL and lists "anti-patterns to NOT carry over"; it refuses third-party URL emission of exact tokens without attestation. — `skills/hallmark/references/design-md.md` and `study.md` (the refusal-layer detail is from design-md.md's table; study.md not read)
- taste-skill's library of directions was built from 247 gallery sites and rebuilt rather than copied (scenes "taken apart... and rebuilt from scratch"). — `CHANGELOG.md`, `SKILL.md`
- NN/g says visual references (moodboards, screenshots of design system, tokens) improve prompts but "won't achieve pixel-perfect results", and a chatbot can describe a reference page's style/layout in text to turn into a prompt. — [NN/g, vague prompts](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/vague-prototyping/)
- Dan Mall's practice of taking "a visual inventory of sites with different approaches to color, concept, and tone" before comps is a primary example of reference gathering for divergence. — [Hooked on Code notes](https://hookedoncode.com/2015/02/so-what-do-i-make-by-dan-mall-notes-from-an-event-apart-talk/)

### Inferences
- A usable reference protocol for the skill (my synthesis from the above, untested): pick references from different categories than the competitors (Mall's "different approaches"), record per reference the structural move and the job it does (not screenshots), say what is NOT carried over (Hallmark), and test each against the product's own facts (taste-skill competitor test).
- footer.design, cta.gallery, 404s.design and bentogrids.com are pattern catalogs of single components; their value is narrow (component-level pattern), and bento grids appear on taste-skill and Hallmark tell lists as a default look ("Bento grid of rounded tiles as the feature section: warned"). So gallery browsing can itself seed generic output.

## Q4. Practitioner reports (2024-2026) on LLMs for UX artifacts: what worked and failed

### Takeaway
The best-sourced report is NN/g's tool test: detailed prompts plus high-fidelity attached artifacts give close-to-human results; vague prompts give generic, template-bound output with wrong patterns; polish can mislead stakeholders. Anthropic's own Claude Design (launched April 2026) is built around design system inference and a handoff bundle to Claude Code, but the evidence about it is vendor claims and a pre-trial blog.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g tested 15+ tools (UX Pilot, Figma First Draft, Uizard, Stitch, Relume; V0, Bolt, Lovable, Replit, Figma Make, Magic Patterns, Subframe; ChatGPT, Claude) on two real scenarios (a profile page for training attendees; a bulk-purchase flow), four single-page prompt types (broad text, detailed text with components and visual style, text plus design artifact, redesign with image of old design), assessed by heuristic evaluation against NN/g designers' own work. — [NN/g testing methodology](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/testing-ai-methodology)
- Worked: "longer prompts with clear, detailed design requirements consistently yield better results"; "The higher the fidelity of the attached file, the more accurately the design was translated" (Figma links best); good for early ideation, stakeholder proofs of concept, quick prototypes for usability tests. — [NN/g, Good from Afar, But Far from Good](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-prototyping/)
- Failed: vague prompts vary widely; even detailed prompts had problems with "visual hierarchy, grouping, color contrast, margin consistency, and overused accent colors" (Bolt placed a course password far from its access link); without style direction designs look "flat and interchangeable" built on Shadcn and Tailwind defaults; a high-level "profile page" prompt produced social-media-style profile headers (wrong pattern) pushing the primary content down; Figma First Draft and Relume force-fit unusual scenarios to templates; high fidelity can shift stakeholder talk to button color before hierarchy is settled. — same article. The article did not report how outputs handled interaction states (page said only that the detailed prompt specified them).
- Prompting advice: "Frankenstein layout" from ambiguity; name an established visual style (neobrutalist) rather than "clean/modern"; attach moodboards/design tokens; supply realistic mock data (JSON) so the tool groups content and sets hierarchy; state content priority; treat a prompt "like creating design specs for handoffs"; have a chatbot critique the prompt for missing context first; "don't confuse visual fidelity with design quality." — [NN/g, Prompt to Design Interfaces](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/vague-prototyping/)
- Flow and redesign findings from NN/g were listed "coming soon" when I read the methodology page. — [NN/g methodology](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/testing-ai-methodology)
- Flow-diagram generators (Whimsical, Eraser, Mermaid etc.) in a Politecnico di Milano thesis sped up early visualization but struggled with contextual understanding and logical coherence; better as reasoning partner. — [thesis page via search](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/handle/10589/246731) (not opened)
- Claude Design (Anthropic Labs, research preview, April 17 2026): reads codebase and design files to build a design system that later projects use; inputs include text, images, DOCX/PPTX/XLSX, codebase, web-capture; "packages everything into a handoff bundle" passed to Claude Code with one instruction. Customer claims: Brilliant "2 prompts" vs "20+" in other tools; Datadog "rough idea to a working prototype before anyone leaves the room." — [Anthropic announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-design-anthropic-labs) (vendor claims)
- Critical commentary on the handoff: the bundle format is unpublished; "The bundle lands cleanly or it lands in a vacuum"; canvas inference can yield "a component mapping that is plausible and wrong"; the author had not yet run the trial; PCWorld reported one session using most of a Pro weekly allowance. — [Ready Solutions](https://readysolutions.ai/blog/2026-04-24-claude-design-handoff-not-canvas/) (a blog, hypothesis-level)
- Figma sources say Figma Make draws on existing component libraries and tokens; no test of output quality found. — [Figma PRD to prototype page](https://www.figma.com/solutions/prd-to-prototype-generator/) (vendor)

### Inferences
- NN/g's findings match the upstream design: the missing ingredients in failures (pattern, content priority, grouping, style) are the things a UX handoff can supply before generation: named pattern for the screen, content hierarchy and groupings, real/mock data, and a named visual style.
- Wrong-pattern failure ("profile page" read as social profile) argues for the handoff naming the job and the pattern class AND the pattern to avoid.
- Treat Claude Design claims as marketing until a neutral trial exists.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve named-designer reports from Lenny's Newsletter, Figma blog, Smashing, or Anthropic's internal design team; searches returned vendor blogs and secondary write-ups. No named account of using Claude/ChatGPT for IA, personas, or journey maps with outcomes. NN/g "AI for UX" flow articles were not available. Note 07 already covers synthetic users.

## Q5. UI-generation benchmarks and studies: what upstream structure improves output

### Takeaway
The academic evidence is thin on "upstream structure". Design2Code shows models fail at recalling visual elements and generating correct layout from screenshots; UICrit shows critique quality can be raised by few-shot and visual prompting. Neither tests a brief-to-UI pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- Design2Code: 484 real webpages, screenshot-to-code with GPT-4o/4V, Gemini and Claude under various prompting methods; models "mostly lag in recalling visual elements from the input webpages and generating correct layout designs." — [arXiv 2403.03163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03163) (abstract only; the page I got did not say which prompting methods helped)
- UICrit: 3,059 critiques and quality ratings for 983 mobile UIs; reports a 55% gain in LLM critique quality via few-shot and visual prompting; authors say LLM critique does not yet match humans; follow-up work and a slide-evaluation study report remaining gaps and limited transfer outside mobile UI. — [UICrit arXiv 2407.08850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08850), [visual prompting follow-up](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.16829), [SlideAudit](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03630) (via search summary)
- UICrit authors suggest the data could train a reward model for generative UI techniques; this is a proposal. — [UICrit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08850)
- A 2026 UXBench paper measures "actionability" of LLM-generated UX critiques. — [arXiv 2606.16262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16262) (title only, not opened)
- Design-skill practice: taste-skill and Hallmark both run a render-and-judge loop (screenshots, rubric; Hallmark's six-axis self-critique); taste-skill states critique must use screenshots "never on the code." — `skills/taste-skill/references/critique.md`, `skills/hallmark/SKILL.md`

### Inferences
- Evidence for "structure improves generation" is practitioner-level (NN/g) and skill-author claims, not benchmarked. A skill that claims specific lifts should say so honestly.
- Self-critique by the generating model carries the same weakness as note 06 observed in designteam-app (self scoring with no anchors); taste-skill's use of screenshots and measured inspection is the stronger design.

### Gaps
- Did not open UICrit/UXBench/Design2Code full texts; no study isolating the effect of a structured brief/IA/state matrix on LLM UI quality was found.
- No benchmark of "anti-generic" skills.

## What an upstream UX handoff should provide so downstream skills produce non-generic UI (consolidated, inferences from the above)

- Surface mode (Persuade/Operate/Read/Experience; or page vs app screen) and the primary job in one sentence. Sources: impeccable SKILL.md, taste-skill SKILL.md.
- Pattern identity: what kind of screen this is and what it is not (NN/g wrong-pattern failure).
- Audience and situation (including state of mind and ambient scene, which impeccable uses to pick light/dark); inferred items labeled as inferred.
- "Uniquely true here" facts and the nearest competitor/neighbor (impeccable `shape`, taste-skill inventory and competitor test).
- Real content with min/typical/max ranges, mock data shaped like the real data, and an explicit list of what does not exist yet so nothing is fabricated (impeccable "Evidence on Hand", Hallmark honest-copy, NN/g mock data).
- Content hierarchy: primary vs secondary, and groupings that must stay together (NN/g Bolt password example).
- Screen inventory with states per screen and transitions (impeccable shape round 2; Hallmark's 8 states for components) so downstream "states" checks have a list to run against.
- Extreme tone words or a pinned direction only when real; otherwise defer visual world selection downstream (impeccable init forbids asking; "The brief wins" for pinned ones). Include anti-goals ("what would feel wrong even if polished").
- Binding constraints: platform, stack, brand assets, accessibility/localization, existing design system (Hallmark, impeccable).
- Open decisions a builder must not invent (impeccable shape item 7).
- Format: a small brief file kept outside shipped source (impeccable: contract "never copied into implementation source"), with semantic-role tokens left to the downstream skill (Hallmark contract).
- Do not hand over fonts/palettes as "replacements" for defaults unless brand-bound (taste-skill tells.md).
