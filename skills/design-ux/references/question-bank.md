# Question bank

Questions experienced designers ask at a kickoff, grouped by what they uncover. Pick the
few that could change a decision in this case; never send the list. Each question should
reach the user with a one-line reason and a recommended answer (see
`designer-stance.md`, "How to ask").

Contents: purpose; risk and history; people and context; past events; hypotheses to react
to; what to research or assume instead.

## Purpose and outcome

- What is going wrong today that made you want this? (A request that names a solution but
  no problem.)
- What was happening when you thought of it? (The trigger situation.)
- What do people do today without it? (The workaround is the real competitor.)
- How will you know it worked, and when would you notice? (No stop condition.)
- What is this not meant to be? (Hidden non-goals.)
- If nothing were built, or a spreadsheet were used, what would be lost? (The option of not
  building.)
- If two of your goals conflict, which wins? Speed or polish? (Silent trade-offs.)

## Risk, constraints, history

- What worries you about this? What is the worst that could happen? (Fears that shape scope.)
- If this failed in six months, what would the reason be? (Failure causes no one has named.)
- What has been tried before, and what happened? (Repeating a past failure.)
- Which constraints are fixed, and why? (Constraints that are really habits.)
- What is already decided about technology or data? (Hidden platform limits.)
- Who else should I talk to, and what should I have asked? (Missing stakeholders.)

## People and context

- Who is this for, and who is it explicitly not for?
- Who decides, who pays, who uses, and who is affected but never asked?
- How much do users differ in skill, frequency, devices and role?
- Where are they, and with what, when they do this? Interrupted? In a hurry?
- How often does it happen: daily, monthly, once?

## Past events (ask the user to recall)

- Tell me about the last time this happened. Walk me through it.
- What were they doing just before, and right after?
- Tell me about a time it went well, and a time it went badly.
- What do they keep on the side to get it done (notes, a spreadsheet, a colleague)?
- What happens when it does not go the normal way?
- What have they tried already, and why did they stop?
- What does the problem cost them now (time, money, mistakes)?
- Which complaints or requests do you hear most often?

Label the answers `[user]`: they are the user's report about their users.

## Hypotheses to react to

- "I am assuming X. Was that true the last three times you saw it?"
- "Which of these two approaches matches how people actually do it, and which would break
  their habit?"
- "What would have to be true for this to work?"

## Research or assume instead of asking

Do not ask the user what you can find out: domain rules and regulations, how comparable
products handle the same problem, platform conventions, accessibility requirements, the
existing code. Research them (`research-and-evidence.md`).

Do not ask what nobody can answer reliably: whether users would like it, use it or pay for
it, or whether it is intuitive. Record these as assumptions with how and by whom they could
be tested.
