## Q1. What do the primary sources actually say (verified base for the lenses)?

### Takeaway
Service blueprint, gulfs, information scent, peak-end, COGA, deceptive patterns, Fogg B=MAP, ISO context of use, application posture, Walter's pyramid, and Microsoft's three principles are confirmed at definition level. Cooper's goal-directed details (perpetual intermediates, excise, represented model, "probable vs possible") and Norman's slips/mistakes definitions are only confirmed via tables of contents or secondary summaries.

### Cited Findings
- Service blueprint: diagram of how people, props and processes relate at touchpoints of one customer journey; one blueprint per journey. Frontstage = in view of the customer; backstage = supporting steps; lines of interaction, visibility, internal interaction. Reveals root causes a single interface review misses, redundancies (e.g. data collected early that staff could reuse), and cross-department handoffs nobody owns. — [NN/g service blueprints](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-blueprints-definition/)
- Service design aims at the employee's experience directly and the customer's indirectly; backstage work can have as much or more impact on UX than visible interactions; frontstage = channels, products, touchpoints, interfaces; backstage = policies, technology, infrastructure, systems. — [NN/g Service Design 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-design-101/)
- Gulf of evaluation = "What state is the system in right now?"; gulf of execution = "What can I do to get where I want, and how?"; execution is only bridged after evaluation is crossed ("any plan based on faulty evaluation is doomed to failure"). — [NN/g two UX gulfs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/two-ux-gulfs-evaluation-execution/)
- Norman's book: principles = discoverability, affordances, signifiers, mapping, constraints, feedback, conceptual models; slips (intent right, action wrong: capture, description-similarity, memory-lapse, mode error) vs mistakes (wrong goal or plan: rule-based, knowledge-based, memory-lapse); forcing functions, interlocks, lock-ins, lock-outs, undo, confirmation. Secondary summaries/TOC only. — [jnd.org book page](https://jnd.org/the-design-of-everyday-things-revised-and-expanded/), [study-guide summary](https://bootcamp.uxdesign.cc/chapter-5-human-error-no-bad-design-1965afd57ce)
- Cooper's four application postures: sovereign (monopolizes attention for long periods), transient (appears, does one job, leaves), daemonic (background, no direct interaction), parasitic/auxiliary (small focused set of functions, persistently visible). Wikipedia citing About Face; primary text not reached; design implications not in source. — [Wikipedia Application posture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application_posture)
- About Face goal-directed process stages: research, modeling, requirements, framework, refinement, support; implementation models vs user mental models treated as a design problem. "Represented model" and "design for the probable" NOT found in any source reached. — [course notes via search](https://enter77.ius.edu/cjkimmer/goal-directed-design/)
- Task analysis: goal vs task distinction; frequency considered to find repetition worth streamlining; novices take more steps than experts; hierarchical task analysis plans capture conditional subtasks (e.g. forgotten password); observe users in their own environment, not only self-report (contextual inquiry, diary studies). NN/g page does not treat criticality directly. — [NN/g task analysis](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/task-analysis/)
- ISO 9241-11 context of use = users, goals/tasks, equipment (hardware, software, materials), environments (physical and social); usability = effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction for specified users in a specified context. Summaries only. — [imbus glossary](https://www.imbus.de/en/glossary/term/context-of-use) (via search), [ISO 9241-11:2018 preview](https://preview.sist.si/sist-preview/63500/33c267a5a7564f298f02bbd65721a181/ISO-9241-11-2018.pdf)
- Information scent: user's imperfect estimate of value a link will deliver; weakened by vague labels ("Learn more"), jargon, hidden context on small screens, unrepresentative images; raised by prior knowledge, social signals; misleading labels erode future scent. — [NN/g information scent](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/)
- Peak-end rule: experiences remembered by peaks and the end; Kahneman cold-water study, ~80% chose the longer trial with the milder end; implications: fix negative peaks, end well, avoid "please don't go" popups, handle outages/dead ends honestly. — [NN/g peak-end](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/peak-end-rule/)
- Walter's hierarchy: functional, reliable, usable, pleasurable (Maslow remap); higher needs depend on lower. Secondary. — [uxdesign.cc review](https://uxdesign.cc/designing-for-emotion-a-book-review-9b73d81be97d), [Qt blog](https://www.qt.io/blog/emotional-design-for-creating-desirable-products)
- Fogg: behavior happens when motivation, ability and prompt converge at the same moment; if it does not occur, at least one is missing; ability = six simplicity factors (time, money, physical effort, mental effort, social deviance, non-routine; labels vary by source); making it easier is usually more sustainable than raising motivation. — [behaviormodel.org](https://www.behaviormodel.org/), [secondary on factors](https://bootcamp.uxdesign.cc/foggs-behaviour-model-a-framework-for-behaviour-change-fd6ce4b0a1f2)
- Deceptive design: 18 types incl. sneaking, forced action, hard to cancel, preselection, obstruction, hidden subscription, hidden costs, trick wording, visual interference, fake social proof/urgency/scarcity, nagging, confirmshaming, comparison prevention, addictive design. — [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types)
- Choice architecture tools (Thaler, Sunstein, Balz 2012): defaults, expect error, give feedback, understand mappings, structure complex choices, incentives; libertarian paternalism. Secondary. — [SSRN Choice Architecture](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2536504)
- Microsoft Inclusive Design principles: recognize exclusion, learn from diversity, solve for one extend to many; persona spectrum permanent/temporary/situational (one arm / arm injury / new parent holding a baby). — [inclusive.microsoft.design](https://inclusive.microsoft.design/), [Inclusive 101 guidebook](https://inclusive.microsoft.design/articles/inclusive-101-guidebook) (via search)
- COGA objectives: understand what things are and how to use them; find what they need; clear content; avoid mistakes and correct them (incl. avoid data loss and timeouts); focus (limit interruptions, short critical paths); do not rely on memory (codes, passwords); help and support; adaptation/personalization. Supplemental to WCAG 2.2. — [W3C COGA Usable](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)
- Hulick: first-run should give a quick taste of success that differs per product (Vimeo: first video upload beats profile completion); attention is a budget; teach inside the product rather than via tours. Secondary interview. — [Appcues interview](https://www.appcues.com/blog/onboarding-new-users-an-interview-with-samuel-hulick)
- Yifrah: microcopy = words tied to user actions (motivation before, instruction during, feedback after); "copy is part of design" is a secondary observation, not found stated by her. — [uxcontent.com course page](https://uxcontent.com/the-microcopy-course/) (via search)

### Inferences
- Cooper's postures give design-time guidance only through inference (e.g. sovereign suits dense, learnable UI; transient suits minimal, fast, no setup); the source reached states definitions only.
- The service blueprint, gulfs, and COGA lists are the best "question-generating" primary material; Fogg/Walter/Hulick are weaker sources and should be cited as frameworks, not evidence.

### Gaps
- No primary text for About Face (excise, perpetual intermediate, represented model, probable/possible) or Norman's exact definitions.
- Criticality is not defined by NN/g; "frequency x criticality x variability" as a triple is not a single published formula in sources reached (treat as synthesis).
- No source for "moments of truth" (Carlzon/Normann) or "a day in the life" reached.
- No source reached for Laws of UX (Hick, Fitts, Jakob), recognition vs recall, progressive disclosure in this round; entries below are [K].
- Hulick "time-to-value" is not confirmed from Hulick himself.

## Q2. Catalogue of 34 lenses (question, blind spot, example, source, stage)

### Takeaway
Lenses cluster into six families: who/why (framing), context and lifecycle, mental models and error, service and multi-actor, emotion/behavior/ethics, and content/structure/prominence. Most valuable at framing: lenses 1-9; at structure: 10-15, 22-24; at screens/states: 16-21, 25-30; at visual direction: 31-34. Stage key: F = problem framing, S = structure, SC = screens/states, V = visual direction.

### Cited Findings
Source tags in the catalogue point to the verified items in Q1; [K] = unverified background knowledge.

### Inferences
(The catalogue is my synthesis; examples are mine.)

**Family A: who and why**

1. Goal vs task (F). Q: What is the person trying to get done in their life, and which tasks here are only means to it? Blind spot: engineers model features and forms; users have goals (e.g. "stop worrying about money" not "enter a transaction"). Ex (budgeting): goal = "know if we can afford the trip", not "categorize expenses". Source: [NN/g task analysis](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/task-analysis/); Cooper goal-directed design [K].
2. Implementation vs mental model (F, S). Q: Is this screen shaped like our database/process or like how the person thinks about it? Blind spot: structure leaks from the schema. Ex (clinic): "appointment slot resources" vs patient's "see Dr. Sato next week after work". Source: [course notes](https://enter77.ius.edu/cjkimmer/goal-directed-design/); Cooper [K].
3. Conceptual model (F, S). Q: What simple story lets someone predict what this does, and does every screen tell the same story? Blind spot: each screen is locally sensible but the whole has no story. Ex (budget): is money "envelopes", "categories with limits", or "a ledger"? Pick one. Source: Norman [jnd.org](https://jnd.org/the-design-of-everyday-things-revised-and-expanded/).
4. Who is the user, really: persona spectrum (F). Q: Which people are permanently, temporarily, or situationally in this mismatch? Blind spot: "average user" is a healthy, focused, young person at a desk. Ex (sign-up): a parent holding a child types one-handed. Source: [Microsoft Inclusive](https://inclusive.microsoft.design/).
5. Recognize exclusion (F). Q: Who does this design quietly leave out, and what assumption did we make about them? Blind spot: exclusion is invisible to the included. Ex (clinic): elderly patient without email cannot receive confirmation links. Source: Microsoft Inclusive principles.
6. Solve for one, extend to many (F, SC). Q: If we solve for the most constrained person, who else benefits? Blind spot: seeing accessibility as a niche cost. Ex: big tap targets and no-timeout forms help the one-handed, the shaky, the hurried. Source: Microsoft Inclusive.
7. Posture (F, S). Q: Is this a place people live in for hours (sovereign), a quick visit (transient), a background worker (daemonic), or a small persistent helper (auxiliary)? Blind spot: engineers default everything to a full-app dashboard. Ex (budget): the daily "log a purchase" is transient; the monthly review is sovereign. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application_posture) (definitions); implications are inference.
8. Perpetual intermediates (F, SC). Q: Who is neither a first-timer nor an expert, and is the design tuned for them? Blind spot: teams design for the demo novice or the power user; most people plateau as intermediates. Ex: a budgeting app 6 months in. Source: Cooper [K, unverified].
9. Probable vs possible (F, SC). Q: What do ~90% do ~90% of the time, and how do we keep the rare case possible without staging it? Blind spot: edge cases get equal screen weight. Ex (clinic): rescheduling is probable; multi-clinic transfer is possible. Source: Cooper [K, unverified]; see also lens 14.

**Family B: context and lifecycle**

10. Context of use (F, SC). Q: Where, with what device, who else is present, how interrupted, under what time pressure? Blind spot: designing for a calm desk. Ex (clinic): receptionist with a phone in one hand and a queue in front. Source: [ISO 9241-11 summary](https://www.imbus.de/en/glossary/term/context-of-use); [NN/g task analysis](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/task-analysis/) (observe in their environment).
11. A day in the life (F). Q: Where does this task sit in the person's day, and what comes right before and after? Blind spot: seeing the app as the centre of life. Ex (budget): the receipt is in a pocket at the checkout, entry happens at night on the sofa with a partner. Source: [K] ethnographic practice; contextual inquiry per NN/g above.
12. Interruption and resumption (SC). Q: If they are pulled away mid-task, what do they find on return? Blind spot: the happy path is uninterrupted. Ex (sign-up): kid interrupts at step 3; is the draft kept? Source: [COGA avoid data loss/timeouts](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/).
13. Lifecycle stages (F, S). Q: What does this look like on day 1, day 30, after a 3-month lapse, at a life change, at leaving? Blind spot: only the populated middle is designed. Ex (budget): returning after 2 months with 200 unreviewed transactions. Source: synthesis [K]; Hulick for first run (below).
14. Frequency, criticality, variability (S, SC). Q: How often, how costly if wrong, how different between users does each task occur? Blind spot: prominence by org chart or build order. Ex (clinic): "check today's list" is daily; "cancel clinic day" is rare but critical, so hidden but protected. Source: frequency and novice/expert variability in [NN/g task analysis](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/task-analysis/); the three-way combination is my synthesis.
15. Offboarding and exit (S, SC). Q: How does someone leave with their data and dignity: export, pause, delete? Blind spot: retention metrics hide the exit. Ex (budget): CSV export, and "delete account" that explains what is erased. Source: ethically [deceptive.design "hard to cancel"](https://www.deceptive.design/types).

**Family C: error, feedback, and clarity (Norman)**

16. Gulf of evaluation. Q: At every moment, can the person tell what state the system is in? Blind spot: state is obvious to the builder. Ex (clinic): is the booking confirmed, pending clinic approval, or just saved? Source: [NN/g gulfs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/two-ux-gulfs-evaluation-execution/). Stage: SC.
17. Gulf of execution. Q: Can they tell what they can do next and how? Blind spot: capability exists but is not discoverable. Ex (budget): how to split one transaction across categories. Source: same. Stage: SC.
18. Signifiers over affordances. Q: What visibly tells someone this is tappable, editable, draggable? Blind spot: "it's a button, it works" ignoring perception. Ex: flat text that is actually a link. Source: [jnd.org](https://jnd.org/the-design-of-everyday-things-revised-and-expanded/). Stage: SC, V.
19. Mapping. Q: Does the arrangement of controls match the arrangement of effects? Blind spot: control placement by implementation. Ex (clinic): calendar columns in the same order as the provider list. Source: Norman; Thaler/Sunstein "understand mappings" [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2536504). Stage: S, SC.
20. Slips vs mistakes. Q: Is this error a wrong action despite right intent (fix the interface) or a wrong plan (fix the model/teaching)? Blind spot: all errors become "validation message". Ex (budget): typing 1500 as 15.00 is a slip; assuming "available" includes savings is a mistake. Source: [study guide](https://bootcamp.uxdesign.cc/chapter-5-human-error-no-bad-design-1965afd57ce) (secondary). Stage: SC.
21. Design for error: undo before confirm. Q: Can this be reversed, and if not, what forces a pause (forcing function)? Blind spot: "Are you sure?" dialogs get ignored. Ex (clinic): cancelling a booking offers 10 seconds of undo; deleting a patient requires typing. Source: Norman TOC [jnd.org](https://jnd.org/the-design-of-everyday-things-revised-and-expanded/); Thaler "expect error" [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2536504); [COGA error objective](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/). Stage: SC.

**Family D: structure and discovery**

22. Information scent. Q: Before tapping, can they guess what they will get, in their words? Blind spot: labels chosen by internal naming. Ex (clinic): "Resources" vs "Doctors and rooms". Source: [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/). Stage: S, SC.
23. Recognition over recall. Q: Does the person need to remember something the screen could show? Blind spot: free-text fields and codes feel "simpler" to build. Ex (sign-up): show the password rules as typing happens; no memorised codes. Source: [COGA memory objective](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/); Nielsen heuristic [K]. Stage: SC.
24. Progressive disclosure vs hiding. Q: What is needed now, what can wait, and can they see that more exists? Blind spot: everything on one screen, or everything hidden. Ex (sign-up): ask only email first; defer profile. Source: [COGA focus](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/); NN/g progressive disclosure [K, not fetched]. Stage: S, SC.
25. Laws as questions (Hick, Fitts, Jakob). Q: How many options compete here; how far/small is the target; does it behave like what they already know? Blind spot: novelty treated as quality. Ex: a nonstandard date picker on a clinic booking. Source: Laws of UX (Yablonski) [K, not fetched]. Stage: SC, V. Use only when a concrete screen exists.

**Family E: service, actors**

26. Beyond the screen. Q: What emails, SMS, phone calls, paper, and staff actions are part of this experience? Blind spot: the app is treated as the whole product. Ex (clinic): reminder SMS and the front-desk reaction to a double booking decide satisfaction. Source: [NN/g service design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-design-101/). Stage: F, S.
27. Frontstage / backstage / line of visibility. Q: Which steps does the user see, and which hidden steps (approval, data entry, review) decide if the promise holds? Blind spot: promising instant results the backstage cannot deliver. Ex (sign-up): "Account ready" while manual verification takes 2 days. Source: [NN/g blueprint](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-blueprints-definition/). Stage: F, S.
28. Who else is affected? Q: Staff, admins, family, caregivers, support: who acts on, sees, or pays for this? Blind spot: single-user assumption. Ex (budget): partner sees your transactions; teen has a restricted view. Source: [NN/g blueprint](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-blueprints-definition/) (employee side); roles x objects idea in note 03. Stage: F, S.
29. Permissions as experience. Q: What does someone see when they cannot do something, and who can fix it? Blind spot: permissions are a backend check, the UI shows a dead end or a 403. Ex (clinic): nurse hits a greyed-out button; does it say who can grant it? Source: inference; [K]. Stage: SC.

**Family F: emotion, behavior, ethics**

30. Emotional temperature. Q: Is the person anxious, ashamed, rushed, or grieving at this moment, and does tone and sequencing respect that? Blind spot: money, health, and failure are treated as neutral data. Ex (budget): over-budget alert wording that does not scold. Source: Walter hierarchy [secondary](https://uxdesign.cc/designing-for-emotion-a-book-review-9b73d81be97d); pleasure only after functional, reliable, usable. Stage: F, SC, V.
31. Trust signals and reliability. Q: At the moment of giving data, money, or health info, what makes this feel safe, and what would break trust? Blind spot: trust is assumed from the logo. Ex (sign-up): say why the phone number is needed and who sees it. Source: Walter's "reliable" tier; [NN/g scent: broken promises erode trust](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/). Stage: SC, V.
32. Peak-end. Q: What are the best and worst moments and the last moment? Blind spot: effort goes to the middle. Ex (sign-up): the end is the first success, not an upsell. Source: [NN/g peak-end](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/peak-end-rule/). Stage: SC.
33. Behavior diagnosis (B=MAP, ability first). Q: If the behavior is not happening, is motivation, ability, or the prompt missing, and which simplicity factor (time, money, effort, thinking, social, routine) is the weakest link? Blind spot: adding persuasion when the real barrier is effort. Ex (sign-up drop-off): not a copy problem but step count and a surprising phone verification. Source: [behaviormodel.org](https://www.behaviormodel.org/), [secondary](https://bootcamp.uxdesign.cc/foggs-behaviour-model-a-framework-for-behaviour-change-fd6ce4b0a1f2). Stage: F, SC.
34. Defaults and dark-pattern check. Q: Whose interest does each default, prompt, and exit path serve, and would the user endorse it if they saw it? Blind spot: growth tactics normalised as best practice. Ex (budget): preticked auto-renew; cancel buried in settings. Source: [deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/types); [choice architecture](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2536504). Stage: SC, V.

**Additional lenses (content, onboarding, trade-offs) rolled up with stage tags**

(Numbered 35-40 here; total 40, within or just above the 25-40 range.)

35. Real content ranges. Q: What is the longest name, the empty case, the 200-item case, the foreign script? Blind spot: lorem ipsum layouts. Ex (clinic): double-barrelled surname, 14 appointments in a day. Source: practice [K]; overlaps note 10. Stage: SC, V.
36. Microcopy as design. Q: What words does the person see before, during and after each action, and who writes them in the design stage? Blind spot: copy is filled in after layout. Ex (sign-up): button says "Create my budget" not "Submit". Source: [uxcontent.com](https://uxcontent.com/the-microcopy-course/) (definition only). Stage: SC.
37. Time to first value. Q: What is the smallest real success the person can have, and how fast? Blind spot: tours and setup before value. Ex (budget): see "left this month" after entering one balance. Source: [Appcues/Hulick](https://www.appcues.com/blog/onboarding-new-users-an-interview-with-samuel-hulick) (first-win per product). Stage: F, SC.
38. Empty states as teaching. Q: What does the first empty screen teach and offer? Blind spot: empty = blank. Ex (clinic): empty schedule shows "add your first provider" with preview. Source: Hulick (teach inside the product); [K]. Stage: SC.
39. Cognitive load and plain language. Q: Could someone tired, anxious, or with low literacy do this without help? Blind spot: designer's fluency. Ex (clinic, elderly): one question per screen with clear next step. Source: [COGA](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/). Stage: SC, V.
40. Design principles as trade-offs ("A even over B"). Q: What are we willing to be worse at to be better at this? Blind spot: principles like "simple and delightful" that choose nothing. Ex (clinic): "Fast for staff even over friendly for patients". Source: NN/g article not reachable (404); format is from Agile Manifesto-style "even over" statements [K]. Stage: F, V.
